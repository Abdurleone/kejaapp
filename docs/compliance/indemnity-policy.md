# KejaApp Indemnity Policy

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy expands on the indemnity clause already in the [Terms of Service §10](terms-of-service.md#10-indemnity) — it explains, in plain terms and grounded in actual Kenyan law, what you indemnify KejaApp against, what that legally means, and what it does not cover. It applies to every registered user (tenant, landlord, agency, mover) and to anyone using KejaApp without an account.

## 2. What "indemnity" means here — and why it isn't a "guarantee"

An indemnity is a **primary, independent promise** to compensate someone for a loss — legally distinct from a **guarantee**, which is a secondary promise to answer for *another person's* debt or default. Kenya's **Law of Contract Act (Cap. 23), section 3(1)** requires a guarantee to be in writing and signed to be enforceable ("no suit shall be brought whereby to charge the defendant upon any special promise to answer for the debt, default or miscarriages of another person unless the agreement… is in writing and signed"). The indemnity in these terms is not a promise to answer for someone else's debt — it's your own, direct, independent obligation to KejaApp for loss KejaApp incurs because of your own conduct. That distinction is why accepting these terms (a click-through agreement, not a separately signed instrument) is legally sufficient for an indemnity in a way it would not be for a guarantee.

## 3. What you indemnify KejaApp against

Per [Terms of Service §10](terms-of-service.md#10-indemnity), you agree to indemnify KejaApp against claims, losses, or damages arising from:

- **Your breach of the Terms of Service**, the [Code of Ethics](code-of-ethics.md), or the [Acceptable Use Policy](acceptable-use-policy.md).
- **Your listing content** — a landlord/agency's property listing, a mover's business profile, or any review, inquiry message, or feedback you submit (see [Terms of Service §4](terms-of-service.md#4-listings-verification-badges-and-content-you-submit)). If your listing infringes someone's rights, misrepresents a property, or is otherwise unlawful, and that exposes KejaApp to a third party's claim, you — not KejaApp — bear that cost.
- **Your conduct toward another user**, online or in person, arising from an interaction KejaApp's platform facilitated (an inquiry, a viewing, a mover service request) but was never a party to (see [Terms of Service §2](terms-of-service.md#2-what-kejaapp-is--and-isnt)).

This mirrors the platform's own structural position: KejaApp is a discovery and coordination layer, not a party to any tenancy, viewing, or moving-service arrangement, and never holds or routes money between users (see the [Payment Boundary](../../README.md#payment-boundary)). The indemnity exists precisely because those arrangements are yours, not KejaApp's — if your conduct in one of them creates liability that lands on KejaApp, this clause is what shifts that cost back to you.

## 4. What this indemnity does not do

Being explicit about limits, since an indemnity clause that reads as unlimited invites exactly the challenge described below:

- **It does not waive any consumer right you cannot lawfully give up.** Kenya's **Consumer Protection Act, 2012 (No. 46 of 2012)** voids several categories of term regardless of what a contract says: **section 4(1)** voids any term purporting to prevent a consumer from joining or bringing a class proceeding; **section 5(3)** voids any provision that negates or varies an implied condition or warranty under that Act or the Sale of Goods Act. This indemnity is not read, and is not intended, to do either.
- **It does not cover KejaApp's own breach of these terms, negligence, or unlawful conduct.** An indemnity clause covers loss arising from *your* conduct — it is not a shield for KejaApp's own wrongdoing, and nothing here should be read as an attempt to make it one.
- **It is read narrowly, not expansively, wherever it's ambiguous.** Under the Consumer Protection Act's **section 7**, "any ambiguity that allows for more than one reasonable interpretation of a consumer agreement provided by the supplier to the consumer… shall be interpreted to the benefit of the consumer." Any genuine ambiguity in this clause resolves in your favor, not KejaApp's, as a matter of statute — not just as a stated intention.
- **It must not be unconscionable or excessively one-sided.** The Act's **section 13** makes it an unfair practice (prohibited outright under **section 15**) to make an "unconscionable representation" — expressly including a consumer transaction that is "excessively one-sided in favor of… someone other than the consumer" or whose terms are "so adverse to the consumer as to be inequitable." This indemnity is scoped to shift back costs genuinely caused by your own conduct, not to make you responsible for KejaApp's ordinary business risk.

## 5. How this interacts with limitation of liability

[Terms of Service §9](terms-of-service.md#9-limitation-of-liability) caps KejaApp's own liability to users; this policy is the mirror image — what you owe KejaApp, not what KejaApp owes you. The two are read together, not as a way to make user liability unlimited while KejaApp's is capped: a claim against you under this policy is still limited to the actual loss KejaApp incurred because of your own breach or conduct, not a general-purpose liability shift.
