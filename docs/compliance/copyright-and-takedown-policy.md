# KejaApp Copyright and Takedown Policy

## 1. Purpose and scope

[Terms of Service §4](terms-of-service.md#4-listings-verification-badges-and-content-you-submit) already requires that you have the rights to any image or content you upload, and licenses what you submit to KejaApp for the purpose of operating the platform. This policy is the enforceable process behind that requirement — what happens when someone claims a listing photo, a mover's business content, or other uploaded material infringes their copyright — grounded in Kenya's **Copyright Act (Cap. 130)**, as amended by the **Copyright (Amendment) Act, No. 20 of 2019**, which specifically added Kenya's online-copyright takedown regime (**section 35B**).

## 2. Why this matters for KejaApp specifically

Property listings live and die by their photos, and a landlord or agency uploading images they don't actually own — a competitor's photos, a stock photo, an old tenant's picture of the unit — is a real, recurring risk pattern for any rental-listing platform, not a hypothetical. [Terms of Service §4](terms-of-service.md#4-listings-verification-badges-and-content-you-submit) already prohibits it and KejaApp's image-fingerprinting system (see the [Acceptable Use Policy §1](acceptable-use-policy.md#1-listings-landlords-agencies)) already catches *reused-on-KejaApp* duplication. This policy covers the case fingerprinting can't: an image copied from *outside* KejaApp entirely, which only the actual rights-holder would recognize.

## 3. How to submit a takedown notice

If you believe content on KejaApp infringes your copyright, send a written notice to `privacy@kejaapp.com` with the subject line "Copyright takedown notice," including:

1. Your full name and contact details.
2. Identification of the copyrighted work you claim is infringed.
3. The specific KejaApp listing, profile, or content location where the infringing material appears (a URL or clear description).
4. A statement that you have a good-faith belief the use is unauthorized.
5. Your signature (a typed full name in an email is sufficient).

This mirrors the requirements Kenya's Copyright Act itself sets for a valid notice under section 35B — a notice missing these elements can't be acted on the same way.

## 4. What happens next

Once a notice meeting the requirements above is received, the specific listing image or content identified is removed or disabled pending review — **within 48 business hours**, matching the same window section 35B sets for internet service providers responding to a valid takedown notice. The account that uploaded it is notified of the takedown and the reason. Repeated, substantiated copyright violations by the same account are treated as a trust-and-safety violation under the same escalation path as any other [Acceptable Use Policy](acceptable-use-policy.md#4-enforcement) violation — logged, reasoned, and counted toward the same suspension/ban threshold.

## 5. Counter-notice

If your own content was taken down and you believe that was a mistake — you do hold the rights, or your use was authorized — reply to the takedown notification with an explanation and any supporting evidence (a license, a permission email, proof you took the photo yourself). This is reviewed the same way any other enforcement action can be contested — see the [Dispute Resolution & Complaints Policy](dispute-resolution-policy.md).

## 6. What this policy doesn't do

- It doesn't make KejaApp a guarantor of any listing's copyright status — [Terms of Service §4](terms-of-service.md#4-listings-verification-badges-and-content-you-submit) and the [Disclaimer](disclaimer.md) already state that landlords/agencies are solely responsible for what they upload having the rights to.
- It doesn't cover trademark disputes (a business name conflict between two movers, for instance) — that's a separate concern from copyright in uploaded content.
- It isn't a substitute for a rights-holder pursuing their own remedies directly against an infringing user under the Copyright Act, if they choose to.

## 7. Relationship to formal legal advice

This policy states KejaApp's honest reading of the notice-and-takedown mechanism Kenya's Copyright Act, as amended in 2019, actually requires. It is **not** legal advice, and has not been reviewed by a lawyer licensed in Kenya. The exact statutory notice requirements were drawn from secondary legal commentary describing section 35B rather than a direct read of the gazetted amendment text (which this pass could not retrieve as a clean, machine-readable copy) — if a real, contested takedown dispute arises, that gap should be closed with a properly sourced copy of the Act before relying on the process above as legally definitive.
